# Andrew Hill Identity Check

Compare the V2.2 Hill phrase against Monk / Barry Harris tendencies.

This document functions as an anti-generic drift check: the Hill engine must not produce output that could pass for Monk or Barry Harris.

---

## Why the Hill Phrase Is Less Tonally Gravitational Than Barry Harris

**Barry Harris tendency:** Harmony moves through 6th–diminished logic; ii–V–I and dominant resolution are central. Guide tones lead to chord tones; cadences punctuate phrases.

**Hill V2.2 phrase:**
- Uses a **harmonic field** (E G Bb C#), not a progression. No Dm7→G7→Cmaj7.
- No guide-tone motion toward a tonic. Pitches orbit the field; they do not resolve to a root.
- No cadence. The phrase ends on cluster and counterline material, not a tonic chord.
- **Verdict:** The Hill phrase is less tonally gravitational than Barry Harris. Field-based, not progression-based.

---

## Why the Hill Phrase Is Less Shell/Punctuation-Based Than Monk

**Monk tendency:** Shell voicings (root, 3rd, 7th); rhythmic gaps; off-beat attacks; chord-as-punctuation; space as material.

**Hill V2.2 phrase:**
- Uses **interval cells and clusters**, not shell voicings. [E3,G3,Bb3] is a Field C subset, not a Cmaj7 shell.
- Rhythm is **layered** (base pulse + delayed entry), not gap-based. Events are distributed across beats, not sparse punctuation.
- No chord-stab silence. The phrase has density and continuity, not Monk-style "wrong note then space."
- **Verdict:** The Hill phrase is less shell/punctuation-based than Monk. More continuous, more field-saturated.

---

## Why the Hill Phrase Is More Field-Based

**Field-based:** Harmony is a region of pitches (E G Bb C#) that sustains. Melody, counterline, and cluster all draw from the same field. Motion occurs by pivot, register shift, and cell transformation — not by chord change.

**Hill V2.2 phrase:**
- Every event cites **source_harmonic_field: Field C**. The field is constant.
- Pitches are subsets of or extensions from the field. No chord symbols; no functional labels.
- **Verdict:** The phrase is explicitly field-based. No progression, no root movement.

---

## Why the Hill Phrase Is More Asymmetrical

**Symmetry:** 4+4, 8+8, or repeated 2+2 patterns.

**Hill V2.2 phrase:**
- Phrase structure is **3+5**. The first group is 3 bars; the second is 5 bars.
- No 4+4. The asymmetry is structural, not incidental.
- **Verdict:** The phrase is more asymmetrical than typical Monk or Barry Harris phrases, which often default to 4- or 8-bar symmetry.

---

## Why the Hill Phrase Is More Rhythmically Layered

**Single layer:** All parts align to one pulse or one metric grid.

**Hill V2.2 phrase:**
- **base_pulse** and **delayed_entry** operate simultaneously. Some events land on the beat; others land on the &.
- Bar 6 provides a **convergence point** — both layers align on beat 0 — before diverging again.
- **Verdict:** The phrase is more rhythmically layered. It has strata, not a single groove.

---

## Summary

| Trait | Barry Harris | Monk | Hill V2.2 |
|-------|--------------|------|-----------|
| Tonal gravity | Strong (ii–V–I) | Moderate (shells, altered) | Weak (field-based) |
| Shell/punctuation | Some | Central | Absent |
| Field-based harmony | No | No | Yes |
| Phrase symmetry | Often 4+4 | Often 4+4 | 3+5 |
| Rhythmic layering | Single pulse common | Gaps, off-beats | Multiple strata |

The V2.2 Hill phrase maintains a distinct identity: field-based, asymmetrical, rhythmically layered, and anti-cadential. It does not drift toward generic modern jazz, Monk, or Barry Harris.
