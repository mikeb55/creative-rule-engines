# Wayne Shorter Engine — V1.0

**Category:** Composition Grammar Engine

## Purpose

Model Wayne Shorter's compositional language as a system of motivic development, harmonic ambiguity, phrase asymmetry, chromatic planing, and narrative form. The engine must generate Shorter-style structural behavior rather than imitate surface jazz clichés.

---

## CORE MUSICAL IDENTITY

Wayne Shorter music combines:

- motivic organization (interval cells)
- non-functional harmony (chromatic planing, modal ambiguity)
- phrase asymmetry (3+5, 5+4, 4+4+3, 7+5, etc.)
- tonal ambiguity (suspended resolution)
- modular phrase chains (narrative form)
- ensemble equality (no pure accompaniment)

The engine must preserve formal signposts while allowing motivic development and harmonic exploration.

---

## CORE RULES

### 1. FORM WITH NARRATIVE ARC

Sections must exist; phrase behavior may vary.

**Allowed structures:**

- 8–16 bar themes
- 12-bar blues with chromatic turnaround
- AABA with altered bridge
- Modular phrase chains (non-repetitive)
- Short complete forms (2–4 min equivalent)

Reject engines that generate endless symmetric phrases.

---

### 2. MOTIVIC HARMONY

Harmony must not default to functional progressions.

**Allowed behaviors:**

- interval-cell sonorities
- chromatic planing (half-step, whole-step)
- modal foundations (Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian)
- suspended resolution
- tritone substitution as structural

Avoid routine ii–V–I movement.

**Resolution may occur through:**

- register change
- density release
- motivic return

---

### 3. MOTIF TRANSFORMATION

Motifs may:

- repeat (exact or near-exact)
- invert
- transpose (minor 3rd, tritone, 4th)
- fragment (subset)
- extend (add notes)
- interrupt (cut off; restart)

**Transformation grammar:**

- repeat
- invert
- transpose
- fragment
- extend
- interrupt

---

### 4. ANGULAR YET LYRICAL THEMES

Themes should combine:

- intervallic leaps (4th, 5th, tritone)
- contour recurrence
- irregular phrase rhythm
- space and silence

Reject themes that behave like scalar bebop lines.

---

### 5. PHRASE ASYMMETRY

The engine must support irregular phrase lengths.

**Examples:**

- 3+5, 5+4, 4+4+3, 7+5
- 3+3+2, 6+6, 5+7
- Delayed/early cadence; overlap; restart

Reject strict 4+4 symmetry as default.

---

### 6. ENSEMBLE EQUALITY

No instrument should function as passive accompaniment.

All parts derive from shared structural material.

**Possible roles:**

- melody
- counterline
- harmonic color
- rhythmic punctuation
- bass (pedal, motivic)

---

### 7. CHROMATIC COLOR

Favor harmonic color over function.

**Preferred behaviors:**

- chromatic planing
- chord extensions (9, #11, ♭5)
- cluster tension points
- modal ambiguity

Avoid overly diatonic or saccharine harmony.

---

### 8. TONAL AMBIGUITY

Multiple possible roots; suspended resolution.

**Techniques:**

- pedal bass with shifting upper structure
- modal interchange without clear tonic
- pitch centricity without functional tonality

---

## OUTPUT TYPES

The engine produces:

- Shorter-style melodic phrases
- Shorter-style harmonic environments
- modular phrase chains
- lead sheet level compositions
- short complete forms
- melody studies

The engine should not attempt full orchestral compositions in early versions.

---

## VALIDATION RULES

Reject output if:

- harmony resolves conventionally too often
- phrases remain symmetrical for extended passages
- ensemble roles collapse into melody + accompaniment
- melodic material lacks motivic identity
- interval logic becomes inconsistent
- output resembles generic jazz language
- GCE < 9.0
