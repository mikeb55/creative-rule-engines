# Wayne Shorter Harmonic Fields

Harmonic fields represent tonal regions and color rather than functional harmony.

Fields are built from interval cells and modal/planing logic.

Avoid traditional tonal cadences.

---

## FIELD A — Modal Dorian

**C D Eb F G A Bb**

**Behavior:**

- Dorian color
- Supports minor 3rd, tritone motifs
- Pedal possible on C

---

## FIELD B — Modal Phrygian

**C Db Eb F G Ab Bb**

**Behavior:**

- Phrygian color
- Chromatic lower neighbor
- Dark, Spanish-tinged

---

## FIELD C — Modal Lydian

**C D E F# G A B**

**Behavior:**

- Lydian #4 color
- Bright but ambiguous
- Supports major 3rd, tritone

---

## FIELD D — Chromatic Planing (Half-Step)

**Root movement:** C → C♯ → D → Eb → E (example)

**Behavior:**

- Parallel structures (e.g., Cm7 → C♯m7 → Dm7)
- Chromatic color
- No functional resolution

---

## FIELD E — Chromatic Planing (Whole-Step)

**Root movement:** C → D → E → F# (example)

**Behavior:**

- Whole-tone flavor
- Planing as structure
- Suspended resolution

---

## FIELD F — Pedal with Shifting Upper Structure

**Pedal:** C (or other)

**Upper structure:** Shifts by half-step, whole-step, or interval cell

**Behavior:**

- Static bass
- Moving harmony above
- Tonal ambiguity

---

## FIELD G — Footprints-Style Turnaround

**Example:** F♯m9(♭5) → F7(♯11) → E9(♭5) → A7(♯9) → Cm7

**Behavior:**

- Chromatic turnaround
- Masked blues structure
- Doubled harmonic rhythm

---

## FIELD MOTION RULES

Harmonic motion occurs by:

- chromatic planing (half-step, whole-step)
- shared pitch pivot
- interval cell mutation
- register shift
- pedal with shifting upper structure

Avoid dominant-tonic resolution.
