# Performance Notes: Shifting Lines

## Style Feel

Shorter narrative with implied two-voice Wyble counterpoint. Dyadic feel, two voices in dialogue. 6/8, ♩. = 72. B minor, AABA.

## Rehearsal Suggestions

- Treat the melody as two implied voices: high line (e.g. D5–F#5) and low line (e.g. B4, E4). Rehearse the dialogue between them.
- The wide leaps (e.g. E4 to G#5) suggest contrary motion; let the space breathe.
- Run the bridge (B) separately—the harmonic shift to Emaj7 and A13 is a clear transition point.
- At A3, cue “Back to dyadic feel” so the return is intentional.

## What the Band Should Listen For

- The two-voice implication: high and low lines in conversation.
- The bridge’s new harmonic colour (Emaj7, A13, E7alt, F#7alt) as a distinct section.
- Phrase slurs and rests as part of the contrapuntal phrasing.

## Where the Bridge Changes Character

**Bars 13–16 (B):** The bridge moves to Emaj7, A13, E7alt, F#7alt. Harmonic colour shifts from B minor narrative to a brighter, more open palette. The chart marks this as “Bridge — clear transition · new harmonic colour.” At bar 17 (A3), the cue “Back to dyadic feel” signals the return to the two-voice dialogue.
