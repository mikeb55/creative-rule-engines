# Wyble Etudes — Shifting Lines

**Source tune:** Shifting Lines (V5.0)  
**Test:** test_shorter_wyble  
**Key:** B minor | **Meter:** 6/8

---

## Section Inspiration

All three etudes derive from the **A section** (bars 1–8) of Shifting Lines:  
Bm9 | Gmaj7 | Em9 | F#7alt | Bm9 | Gmaj7 | Em9 | F#7alt

The dyadic motif (D–F#, B–E, G#–E, etc.) and high–low contrary motion from the tune shape the two-voice counterpoint.

---

## Etude 1 — Contrary Motion Dyads

**Concept:** Upper and lower voices move in opposite directions. Each dyad pairs a melodic note with a bass note outlining the chord.

**Practice suggestions:**
- Feel the 6/8 pulse: two dotted quarters per bar.
- Play each voice separately; the lower voice should imply stepwise bass motion.
- Practice the D–F#–B and B–E–G dyadic pairs in isolation.
- Use a slow tempo (♩. = 50–60) for clarity.

---

## Etude 2 — Voice Crossing and Interval Expansion

**Concept:** Voices may cross; intervals expand from thirds to sixths and tenths.

**Practice suggestions:**
- Identify voice crossings (e.g. B4 in upper voice vs G4 in lower).
- Work on interval expansion: 3rd → 6th → 10th in the same harmonic context.
- Keep both voices audible when they cross.
- Use fingerstyle to maintain clear articulation.

---

## Etude 3 — Moving Bass with Melodic Upper Voice

**Concept:** Active stepwise bass in 6/8 supports a lyrical upper melody.

**Practice suggestions:**
- Learn the bass line first; it should outline roots, 3rds, 5ths.
- Add the melody above; the upper voice carries the tune’s melodic fragments.
- Practice with a metronome on the dotted quarter.
- Aim for a singing quality in the upper voice.

---

## General Guitar Practice

- **6/8 feel:** Emphasise beats 1 and 4; the middle eighth notes are lighter.
- **Fingerings:** Choose positions that minimise hand jumps.
- **Dynamics:** Try *p* for one voice and *mf* for the other to hear independence.
