# Wyble Target Etudes — Notes

**Source:** Big Ten validation pieces (V6.0)  
**Style:** Jimmy Wyble two-voice counterpoint for solo guitar  
**Created:** 2026-03-11

## Overview

These etudes are bar-targeted counterpoint studies derived directly from the four Big Ten validation pieces. Each tune yields three 8-bar etudes, each derived from specific bars of the composition. The etudes reinforce the source material while developing Wyble-style two-voice technique.

## Narrative Drift (C minor, 4/4, ♩ = 132)

**Source:** test_shorter_scofield — Wayne Shorter Narrative + Scofield–Holland Groove

### Etude A — Contrary motion dyads
- **Bars used:** 1–4 (Cm9 → Abmaj7 → Bb7 → Ebmaj7)
- **Concept:** Upper voice carries melodic fragments (Eb5–G5–Bb5–D5–Eb5, etc.); lower voice provides stepwise bass (C–Eb–G–Bb, Ab–C–Eb–G, etc.). Voices move in contrary motion.
- **Practice:** Play slowly; ensure each dyad is clearly voiced. Focus on clean finger independence. Use alternate picking or fingerstyle.

### Etude B — Voice crossing and interval expansion
- **Bars used:** 9–12 (Cm9 → Abmaj7 → Bb7 → Ebmaj7)
- **Concept:** Deliberate voice crossings (e.g. G4 melody with G5 bass) and wider interval spans. Upper voice expands; lower voice crosses above at phrase points.
- **Practice:** Identify crossing points; practice the hand shifts. Maintain legato where possible.

### Etude C — Moving bass with melodic upper voice
- **Bars used:** 17–20 (Cm9 → Abmaj7 → Bb7 → Ebmaj7)
- **Concept:** Active stepwise bass (C–Eb–G–Bb, Ab–C–Eb–G) under the Scofield-style melodic fragments. Bass implies harmony; melody retains contour.
- **Practice:** Isolate the bass line first; add melody. Emphasise the groove.

## Shifting Lines (B minor, 6/8, ♩. = 72)

**Source:** test_shorter_wyble — Wayne Shorter Narrative + Wyble Linear Counterpoint

### Etude A — Contrary motion dyads
- **Bars used:** 1–4 (Bm9 → Gmaj7 → Em9 → F#7alt)
- **Concept:** Two-voice dyads in 6/8. Upper: D5–F#5–B4, B5–E5–G4, E4–G#5, A5–F#5. Lower: stepwise bass outlining each chord.
- **Practice:** Feel the 6/8 pulse; avoid accenting every eighth. Let the dotted rhythms breathe.

### Etude B — Voice crossing and interval expansion
- **Bars used:** 9–12 (Bm9 → Gmaj7 → Em9 → F#7alt)
- **Concept:** Voice crossings (E4 vs G#5, G#5 vs E4) and expanded intervals. Two-voice implication preserved.
- **Practice:** Mark crossing points; practice the transitions. Keep voices distinct.

### Etude C — Moving bass with melodic upper voice
- **Bars used:** 17–20 (Bm9 → Gmaj7 → Em9 → F#7alt)
- **Concept:** Moving bass (B–D–F#, G–B–D, E–G–C#, F#–A–C#) under the Wyble-style melodic line.
- **Practice:** Bass drives the harmony; melody floats above. Work on tone separation.

## Quiet Orbit (D major, 4/4, ♩ = 44)

**Source:** test_frisell_counterpoint — Frisell Atmosphere + Counterpoint / Tonality Hybrid

### Etude A — Contrary motion dyads
- **Bars used:** 1–4 (Dmaj9, Gmaj7, Em7)
- **Concept:** Sparse, spacious material. F#5 sustained; B4–D6 wide leap; bass (D–F#–A–D, G–B–D–F#, E–G–B–E) moves stepwise.
- **Practice:** Very slow tempo. Focus on tone and sustain. Let rests breathe.

### Etude B — Voice crossing and interval expansion
- **Bars used:** 9–12 (Dmaj9, Gmaj7, Em7)
- **Concept:** D5 in “bass” voice crosses above B4 melody; B4–D6 wide spacing retained. Interval expansion in sparse texture.
- **Practice:** Identify the crossing; practice the hand position. Maintain Frisell-like spaciousness.

### Etude C — Moving bass with melodic upper voice
- **Bars used:** 17–20 (Dmaj9, Gmaj7, Em7)
- **Concept:** Active stepwise bass (D–F#–A–D, G–B–D–F#, E–G–B–E) under F#5–A5, B4–D6, G5. Bass implies harmony; melody remains sparse.
- **Practice:** Bass leads; melody responds. Keep the pacing slow and reflective.

## Northern Thread (Eb major, 4/4, ♩ = 84)

**Source:** test_metheny_bacharach — Metheny–Bacharach Lyrical Architecture + Wyble Linear Counterpoint

### Etude A — Contrary motion dyads
- **Bars used:** 1–4 (Ebmaj7 → Cm7 → Fm7 → Bb7)
- **Concept:** Lyrical upper voice (G5–Bb5–C6, Bb5–G5, A5–C6, D5–G5); stepwise bass (Eb–G–Bb, C–Eb–G–C, F–A–C–Eb, Bb–D–F).
- **Practice:** Sing the melody; match the guitar. Focus on voice-leading elegance.

### Etude B — Voice crossing and interval expansion
- **Bars used:** 9–12 (Ebmaj7 → Cm7 → Fm7 → Bb7)
- **Concept:** Bb5 melody with C6 “bass” — voice crossing. Lyrical contour preserved; intervals expand.
- **Practice:** The crossing should feel natural, not forced. Maintain the Bacharach-style lyricism.

### Etude C — Moving bass with melodic upper voice
- **Bars used:** 17–20 (Ebmaj7 → Cm7 → Fm7 → Bb7)
- **Concept:** Moving bass (Eb–G–Bb, C–Eb–G–C, F–A–C–Eb, Bb–D–F) under the G5–Bb5–C6 melodic arc.
- **Practice:** Bass outlines the harmony; melody sings. Work on legato and phrasing.

## General Practice Suggestions

1. **Slow first:** Learn each etude at half tempo. Ensure both voices are clear.
2. **Voice isolation:** Practice upper and lower voices separately before combining.
3. **Contrary motion:** When both voices move, aim for contrary motion; avoid parallel fifths/octaves.
4. **Dyads over chords:** Think in dyads (two-note units), not block chords.
5. **Apply to tunes:** After mastering an etude, improvise similar counterpoint over the corresponding section of the source tune.
