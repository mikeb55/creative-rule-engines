# Jazz Guitar Composition Guidelines

## Purpose

Quality guidelines for jazz guitar composition. These are **approaches that support excellence**, not restrictive rules. Experimentation is encouraged when it serves the music.

---

## Guitar-First Considerations

### Playability Awareness
- Consider guitar playability when writing for guitar
- Prefer idiomatic voicings over piano-derived spacing
- **But:** Break these when experimentation serves the music

### Trio Context Awareness
- In guitar trio format, consider that bass and drums are present
- Avoid overfilling space that bass/drums should occupy
- **But:** Full arrangements can work when intentional

### Physical Economy
- Efficient hand movement supports fluency
- Position-based thinking supports navigation
- **But:** Wide stretches and position shifts work when they serve the music

---

## Polyphony & Line Approaches (Jazz Guitar Trio)

### Single-Note Line Preference
- In jazz guitar trio, single-note lines often create clearer polyphony
- Independent melodic lines create interaction
- **But:** Block chords, shell voicings, and stacked harmony work when appropriate

### Voice Independence
- When writing polyphony, consider voice independence
- Each voice should have its own character
- **But:** Parallel motion and stacked harmony can be powerful when intentional

### Chordal vs. Linear
- Linear approaches often work well in trio format
- Chordal approaches can work when they serve the music
- **But:** No single approach is required

---

## Diminished & Double-Time Approaches

### Diminished Material
- Diminished material often functions as pivot or connector
- Can create smooth voice-leading
- **But:** Diminished material can be decorative when it serves the music

### Double-Time (DT)
- DT is often defined by harmonic velocity, not just speed
- Limited DT dosage often creates better pacing
- **But:** Extended DT works when it serves the music

### Swing & Clarity
- Swing feel and clarity often support DT passages
- **But:** Other feels work too

---

## Structural Approaches

### Solo Development Arc
- 3-chorus arc (establish → develop → resolve) often works well
- **But:** Other approaches (1 chorus, 5 choruses, free form) work too

### Texture Floor Awareness
- 2 voices = common default
- 3 voices = often intentional
- 4 voices = often brief and earned
- **But:** Dense textures work when they serve the music

---

## MethodBook & Style Considerations

### Bebop Language
- Bebop language often supports groove, harmonic velocity, or lineage
- **But:** Bebop language can be used anywhere when it serves the music

### Style-Specific Exclusions
- Some tunes (The Mirror, Crystal Silence, Harmolodic Sketch, Fractured Light) often work better without traditional bebop language
- **But:** These are suggestions, not requirements

---

## Quality Guidelines (Not Restrictions)

### When to Apply These
- Use these guidelines when writing jazz guitar music
- Consider them as quality checks, not creative constraints
- Break them when experimentation serves the music

### When Not to Apply
- These guidelines are jazz guitar-specific
- Don't apply to orchestral, songwriting, or chamber work
- Other styles have their own appropriate techniques
