# Jazz Style Guardrails

## Purpose

Jazz-specific composition guidelines that complement universal principles. These are **quality approaches for jazz**, not restrictive rules. Experimentation is encouraged when it serves the music.

---

## Bebop Language Usage

### When Bebop Works Well
- Bebop language often supports groove, harmonic velocity, or lineage
- Traditional bebop works well in bebop contexts
- **But:** Bebop can be used anywhere when it serves the music

### Style Considerations
- Some tunes (The Mirror, Crystal Silence, Harmolodic Sketch, Fractured Light) often work better without traditional bebop language
- These styles often prioritize space, texture, and modern harmony
- **But:** These are suggestions, not requirements — use bebop if it serves the music

---

## Jazz Voice-Leading Approaches

### Chromatic Approach Tones
- Chromatic approaches often create smooth voice-leading
- Half-step approaches often serve the harmony
- **But:** Not every chord needs chromatic approaches — use when appropriate

### Tritone Substitution
- Often creates smooth bass motion
- Can serve harmonic flow
- **But:** Functional harmony works too — use what serves the music

### Diminished Passing Chords
- Often function as connectors between chords
- Can create smooth voice-leading
- **But:** Can be omitted if not needed — use when appropriate

---

## Jazz Voicing Approaches

### Shell Voicings
- Root-3rd-7th often provide clarity
- Physical economy (minimal hand movement) often supports fluency
- **But:** Full voicings work too — use what serves the music

### Drop-2, Quartal, Open Voicings
- Different voicings create different colors
- Use when they serve the music
- **But:** No single voicing type is required — experiment freely

### Voice-Leading
- Smooth voice-leading often creates flow
- Common tones and stepwise motion often work well
- **But:** Leaps can be effective when intentional — use what serves the music

---

## Jazz Form & Development Approaches

### Standard Forms
- AABA, 12-bar blues, 32-bar song form are common
- **But:** Non-standard forms are encouraged — use what serves the music

### Solo Development
- 3-chorus arc often works (establish → develop → resolve)
- **But:** Other approaches work too — 1 chorus, 5 choruses, free form, whatever serves the music

### Head Arrangements
- Clear head (melody) is common
- **But:** Headless pieces work too — use what serves the music

---

## Jazz Rhythm & Groove Approaches

### Swing vs. Straight
- Swing is traditional, straight 8ths are modern
- **But:** Mixing feels works — use what serves the music

### Pocket & Time
- Locking with rhythm section often works well
- **But:** Rubato and free time work too — use what serves the music

### Syncopation
- Syncopation often creates groove and interest
- **But:** On-beat playing can be powerful too — use what serves the music

---

## Jazz Harmony Approaches

### Extended Chords
- 9ths, 11ths, 13ths often add color
- **But:** Triads and 7th chords work perfectly — use what serves the music

### Altered Dominants
- b9, #9, #11, b13 often create tension
- **But:** Natural 9ths and 13ths work too — use what serves the music

### Modal Interchange
- Borrowing from parallel modes often adds color
- **But:** Diatonic harmony is beautiful — use what serves the music

---

## Experimental Jazz

### Breaking Conventions
- Breaking conventions is encouraged when it serves the music
- Experimental harmony, form, and rhythm are valid
- **But:** Know what you're breaking and why — intentional experimentation works best

### Quality Still Matters
- Even experimental jazz benefits from clarity of intent
- Development and form still apply (even if non-traditional)
- Space and contrast still create impact
- **But:** Experimentation is encouraged — these are guidelines, not restrictions

## Double-Time Excellence (DTE)

### Framework Reference
- **Complete DTE Framework:** See `dte-framework.md`
- Comprehensive evaluation system for jazz double-time material
- 10-point scoring system with stylistic anchors (Parker, Martino, Wyble)
- Applies to DT generation, critique, and scoring

### DTE-ARC Framework
- **Multi-Chorus Solo Evaluation:** See `dte-arc-framework.md`
- Evaluates complete solo arcs (especially 3-chorus solos)
- Integrates DTE scores for DT sections with overall structural assessment
- Chorus-by-chorus evaluation with DT dosage rules

### Key Principles
- DT = perceptual velocity within swing grammar (not just speed)
- Rhetoric outranks density in DT contexts
- Making the changes = audible resolution points, not scale choice
- DT dosage: ~25–30% of solo, back-loaded, never 100% of a chorus
- DT bars must be bebop sentences with breath + resolution, not continuous runs
